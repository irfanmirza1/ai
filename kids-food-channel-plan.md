# Kids Food Channel: Research and Launch Plan

*Research date: 23 September 2026. Policies and prices change often, so check the linked sources before you rely on any number.*

---

## 1. Summary

1. **Food with recurring characters is the best choice of your options.** It gives you an endless supply of topics (every food, every country, every lunchbox), it links naturally to a website (recipes and printables), and it lets you make a second, better-paying stream of content aimed at **parents**.
2. **Your biggest risk is YouTube's policy, not the production tools.** In July 2026 YouTube clarified its "inauthentic content" rules. Videos that are made from a template, feel interchangeable, or are AI clips with no clear story **cannot earn ad money**. Kids' AI content is under the most pressure of any category right now (details in section 2).
3. **Build two content streams from each episode:**
   - **Stream A: animated character episodes** (Made for Kids, on YouTube and YouTube Kids). These build the brand and reach a lot of kids, but they earn very little from ads.
   - **Stream B: short recipe and lunchbox videos for parents** (*not* Made for Kids). These go on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and Pinterest. Ads pay better, comments are allowed, and this is what brings people to your website.
4. **Flow credits will limit how much you can make.** 1,000 credits is about 50 clips from Veo 3.1 Fast, which is roughly 3 minutes of usable footage per account per month. Plan your production around that (section 4).
5. **Using several Google AI Pro accounts to pool credits is risky.** Google's policies prohibit getting around usage limits with multiple accounts. At minimum, **never run your YouTube channel from one of the accounts you use for generation** (section 2.4).

## 2. What changed in 2025–2026

### 2.1 YouTube "inauthentic content" (monetization)

- In July 2025, YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content". In **13–16 July 2026** it clarified the policy into three categories that **cannot be monetized**:
  1. **Generic or repetitive content.** Videos that look made from a template, with little variation between them, low educational value and no real creator input. The examples YouTube gives include *templated storylines* and AI material with no creator perspective.
  2. **Unsatisfying or off-putting content.** Emotionally manipulative formulas, stories that feel interchangeable, content made only to shock, *repetitive distress scenarios*, and *AI clips that lack a coherent narrative*.
  3. **AI personas giving advice on sensitive topics** (health, legal, finance). This is relevant to you: don't have a cartoon "nutritionist" give health claims.
- YouTube says its rules don't depend on which tool you use. AI-assisted content is allowed when it is original and has value.
- **Reported enforcement:** a Bible-stories kids channel (about 588K subscribers) was demonetized across the whole channel in early 2026. A third-party report also says an AI kids channel earning about $4.25M a year was taken down in April 2026. Both reports come from third-party blogs, not from YouTube.

### 2.2 Kids-specific pressure

- **1 April 2026:** more than 200 child-development experts and advocacy groups wrote to Google and YouTube asking them to stop recommending AI "slop" to children.
- **YouTube's response:** it is *"limiting AI-generated content in the [YouTube Kids] app to a small set of high-quality channels."* So getting into YouTube Kids with AI content is now a quality bar you have to clear. It is not automatic.
- YouTube's older **quality principles for kids' content** still apply. Channels made up mostly of low-quality kids' content can be removed from the Partner Program. What YouTube rewards: content that is age-appropriate and enriching, encourages healthy habits, and supports learning and curiosity.

### 2.3 "Made for Kids" economics

- You earn only from contextual ads. Reported RPM is roughly **$0.50–$2**, and some reports are lower.
- You lose **comments, the notification bell, end screens, cards, memberships and Super Thanks**.
- **What this means for you:** the animated episodes are for building the brand. The money comes from Stream B, the website, printables, and later books and merch.

### 2.4 Google Flow / AI Pro

| Item | What's true now |
|---|---|
| AI Pro | About $19.99/month and **1,000 Flow credits/month** |
| Veo 3.1 Lite | About 10 credits per clip |
| Veo 3.1 Fast | About 20 credits per clip |
| Veo 3.1 Quality | About 100 credits per clip |
| Clip length | 4, 6 or 8 seconds |
| Visible watermark | Since August 2026, Google lets you switch it off in *Settings → Media Watermark*. The invisible **SynthID** watermark and C2PA metadata always stay. |
| Character consistency | "Ingredients to Video" takes up to 3 reference images (character, object, scene). Put the character first. |
| Native voices | Lip sync is good for short lines, but **there is no way to keep the same voice across clips**. Long lines drop words, and scenes with several speakers are unreliable. |

**Multiple accounts:** Google's Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy and Terms don't allow multiple accounts used to get around usage limits. The penalty can be restriction or suspension of the whole Google account. Things you can do about it:
- Keep the **YouTube channel on its own Google account** (a Brand Account owned by an account you never use for generation). Then a suspension on a generation account can't take the channel down.
- Legitimate ways to get more credits: Google AI Ultra, buying credit top-ups, or paying per clip through the Veo API.
- **I can't tell you that your setup is allowed.** Read the current terms yourself.

### 2.5 Jogg AI

- **What it does:** turns a character image (it supports cartoons, animals and illustrations) into a **talking video with lip sync**. It has 700+ voices, voice cloning and 50+ languages.
- **Pricing:** from about $29/month (Starter). The higher tiers have limits on video credits and on length per video (some tiers cap videos at 1 minute).
- **Where it fits:** use it for **dialogue shots**, where it keeps each character's voice the same in every episode. This fixes Veo's biggest weakness.
- **Where it doesn't fit:** it was built for marketing and avatar videos, so the motion is mostly the face and head. Use Veo for action scenes.
- **Tip:** give every character one fixed voice. You can generate the voice in Jogg (or ElevenLabs) and **mute Veo's generated voices**. Keep Veo's sound effects and ambience only.

### 2.6 Other platforms

- **TikTok and Instagram are for users 13 and older.** The under-13 TikTok experience is very restricted, and TikTok paid a $400M COPPA settlement. **Don't make content aimed at kids for these platforms. Aim it at parents.**
- **YouTube auto-dubbing:** since February 2026 it is free for eligible creators in 27 languages. Channels that add dubbed audio tracks report getting 25% or more of their watch time from other languages. **Turn it on.** Food and cartoon characters translate well.
- **TV and FAST channels** (streaming TV channels that are free and paid for by ads) come later, once you have 50 or more episodes. **Warning:** Kidoodle.TV began liquidation on 31 August 2026, so check any distributor's finances before you sign.

## 3. Choosing the concept

### 3.1 Your 5 original concepts, scored honestly

| Concept | Risk of looking like "AI slop" | Link to the website | Pay from parent-facing content | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🦊 Curious Fox | Medium | Weak | Weak | A generic premise. It's hard to stand out. |
| 🔬 Little Science Lab | Low, if the facts are accurate | Good (experiments, printables) | Good | Strong, but every fact must be checked. |
| 🌍 Passport Kids | Low | Good | Good | Strong, but needs real footage and research. |
| 🌙 Dreamland Adventures | **Very high.** AI bedtime stories are the most flooded format. | Weak | Weak | **Avoid.** |
| 🕵️ Dino Detective | Medium | Medium | Weak | Fun, but only for kids. There's no parent side. |
| 🥕 **Food characters (recommended)** | Low to medium, **if there's a real recipe and a lesson** | **Excellent** (recipes, printables, lunchbox ideas) | **Excellent** (recipe and lunchbox videos) | **Best overall** |

### 3.2 The recommended format

Combine your three best food ideas into **one world with one cast**:

- **Cast:** Broccoli Bob, Carrot Coco, Tomato Tim, Potato Pete and Corny. They live in a garden kitchen.
- **Three repeating segments** give variety without becoming a template:
  1. **Story (60–70% of the episode):** a small problem that one character solves.
  2. **"What can we make?" (3 ingredients):** the real recipe.
  3. **Food Passport:** sometimes the crew travels to a country, learns one food, one fact and one word.
- **Content to avoid:**
  - Characters who are scared of being eaten.
  - Characters in pain or distress, or "self-eating" gags. YouTube specifically lists "repetitive distress scenarios" as off-putting content.
- **A better version of the Broccoli Bob episode:** *"Broccoli Bob Wants a Superpower."* The crew shows him everything broccoli can become (soup, pasta, a "tiny trees" salad). He's proud, not afraid.
- **Child safety:**
  - **Don't show popcorn, whole grapes, whole nuts or hard raw carrot pieces as snacks for toddlers.** These are standard choking-hazard foods for children under 4.
  - Always show an adult handling knives, the stove and the oven.
  - Add allergy notes (nuts, egg, dairy, gluten) to the recipe cards.
- **The strongest way to show real human input:** film the recipe segment yourself, with **real hands, a real kitchen and a real result**, even if it's shot on a phone. It proves the content is original, it's something AI clip channels don't do, and the same footage becomes your Stream B Shorts.

### 3.3 Working names

Veggie Crew Kitchen · The Garden Kitchen Gang · Tiny Tummy Tales · Crunchy Crew

Before you commit to a name:
- Check that the YouTube handle, the .com domain and the Instagram and TikTok handles are all free.
- Search a trademark database (USPTO or WIPO).
- Keep clear of VeggieTales and Vegesaurs.

## 4. Production pipeline

### 4.1 One-time setup (weeks 1–2)

1. **Character sheet for each character** (Flow's image generation, Nano Banana or Whisk):
   - front, side and back views
   - 6 expressions
   - 3 poses
   - one fixed style line that goes in every prompt, for example *"soft 3D clay style, pastel kitchen, warm light"*
2. **Sets:** garden kitchen, fridge, market and a "passport portal". Generate each one once and reuse it as an ingredient image.
3. **Voices:** one voice per character in Jogg or ElevenLabs. Save the voice IDs and keep them the same forever.
4. **Brand assets:** logo, thumbnail template (the character's face, the food, 2–3 words), intro jingle of 5 seconds or less, and an outro card.

### 4.2 Each episode (target 4–6 minutes)

| Step | Tool | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Script and beats (written by you) | Your own writing, with AI help for ideas | Hook in the first 10s → problem → journey → recipe → lesson → recap song |
| Dialogue shots | Jogg: character frame + voice line | 8–15 talking shots |
| Action shots | Flow: Ingredients to Video (character + set + prop) | 10–20 clips |
| Recipe segment | Your phone, real hands | 60–90s of real footage |
| Edit | CapCut or DaVinci Resolve (free) | Captions, SFX, music from YouTube Audio Library or licensed sources |
| Shorts | Cut from the episode | 3–4 vertical clips |
| Parent recipe video (Stream B) | Recipe footage + 1 character cameo | 1 Short/Reel plus a pin |
| Languages | YouTube auto-dub | Up to 27 audio tracks |

### 4.3 Credit budget (estimates)

- 1,000 credits ÷ 20 per Veo 3.1 Fast clip = **50 clips**, each up to 8 seconds.
- If you keep about **half** of them (the rest have mistakes), that's **about 25 clips, or about 3 minutes of action footage per account per month.**
- **Ways to save credits:**
  - Test prompts with **Lite** (10 credits). Use **Fast** only for final shots. Use **Quality** (100 credits) only for thumbnail and hero shots.
  - Put dialogue in Jogg, not Veo.
  - Reuse establishing shots and sets across episodes.
- **Realistic output with one account:** about 2 episodes a month plus about 8 Shorts. That's a sustainable pace. Consistency matters more than volume, and uploading at high volume is exactly what triggers "mass-produced" flags.

## 5. Cross-platform plan

| Platform | Audience | What to post | Made for Kids? |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube main channel | Kids and families | Full episodes, compilations (only as a small part of uploads), character Shorts | **Yes** |
| YouTube Kids | Kids | Gets your videos automatically if you meet the quality bar | (automatic) |
| YouTube (same or second channel) | **Parents** | "3-ingredient kids' snacks", "lunchbox ideas", "how to get kids to eat veggies" | **No** → comments allowed, better RPM |
| Instagram Reels | Parents | Recipe and bento Reels with a character cameo | Aimed at 13+ |
| TikTok | Parents | Same as Instagram, plus trending sounds | Aimed at 13+ |
| Facebook Reels | Parents and grandparents | Same as Instagram | – |
| **Pinterest** | **Parents, especially mothers who plan meals** | Recipe pins and printable pins linking to the website | **The biggest driver of website traffic** |
| Later: FAST/TV, book deals | Families | Libraries of 50+ episodes | – |

**Rule:** anything aimed at *kids* goes only to YouTube. Everything else is framed for *parents*.

**On a separate parent channel:** it's cleaner to keep Made for Kids episodes and parent recipe videos on separate channels, because otherwise it can confuse how YouTube handles your videos. A good sequence is to start both streams on one channel, then split them once the parent videos find an audience.

## 6. Website

**Purpose:** a hub for parents. It builds an email list and holds the products.

**Pages:**
- **An episode page for each episode:** the embedded video, the recipe card (with Recipe schema markup so it can show in Google recipe results), one fun fact and a free printable.
- **Recipes library:** filter by age, number of ingredients, lunchbox, and country.
- **Printables:** character colouring pages, "try a new veggie" sticker charts, recipe cards.
- **Email signup:** "Get a free weekly lunchbox plan". This is the list you own, so it's the most important page.
- **Shop (later):** printable bundles, and your own storefront or links to Etsy, TpT (Teachers Pay Teachers) and Gumroad.

**Revenue, from soonest to latest:**
1. Printable packs on Etsy, TpT and Gumroad
2. Affiliate links to kids' kitchen tools
3. Display ads once traffic qualifies
4. Recipe and activity books on Amazon KDP
5. Sponsorships from food and kitchen brands (these need a parent audience)
6. Merch and plush toys

**Privacy:**
- Write the site **for parents**, not kids.
- Don't collect data from children (COPPA).
- Keep ad trackers off any page aimed at kids.

**Tech:** a simple static site (Astro or Next.js) or WordPress with a recipe plugin. This repository is a good place to build it next.

## 7. 90-day launch plan

| Weeks | Goals |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Name and handles, character bible, voices, 2 sets, thumbnail template, separate Google account for YouTube |
| 3–4 | Make **3 episodes** before launching, and 12 Shorts. Record the recipe segments. Set up a one-page website with email signup. |
| 5–8 | Launch. Post 1 episode a week and 3–4 Shorts a week, and parent Reels and TikToks 3 times a week. Post 5 pins a week. |
| 9–12 | Check your numbers (below). Double down on the segment that performs best (Story, 3-Ingredient or Passport). Release the first printable pack. Turn on auto-dub. |

**Numbers to watch:**
- Average view duration (AVD) on episodes: aim for 40% or more
- Click-through rate (CTR) on Shorts
- Parent Shorts that bring website clicks
- Email signups a week
- Which character gets requested most (move them to the front of the thumbnails)

**YouTube Partner Program:** needs 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views in 90 days. Check the current thresholds.

## 8. Checklist to stay monetizable

- [ ] Every episode has a **unique story** with a beginning, middle and end. No fill-in-the-blanks template.
- [ ] Every episode teaches something **real and correct**: a recipe, a fact or a word.
- [ ] You write the script and direct the story yourself. Keep your drafts as evidence of human input.
- [ ] You film your own recipe footage and record or direct the voices yourself.
- [ ] The same characters and voices appear in every episode.
- [ ] No distress, pain, fear-of-being-eaten or shock formats.
- [ ] No copyrighted characters, songs or brands.
- [ ] No health or medical claims from characters.
- [ ] Say in the description that the video is AI-assisted. It isn't required for obvious cartoons, but it builds trust with parents.
- [ ] Upload at a pace you can keep up with good quality, not as many as you can.

## 9. Earnings estimate (added after vidIQ research, 23 Sep 2026)

**Rate used:** vidIQ's calculator gives about **$1.50 per 1,000 views** for a general English kids/entertainment channel with 40% Shorts views. Made for Kids videos usually earn less, about **$0.50–$1.50 per 1,000 views**. The parent recipe videos (not Made for Kids, US audience, Food category) come out at about **$2 per 1,000 views**.

| Stage | Monthly views | YouTube ads | Website, printables, sponsors | Total per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Months 1–3 | under 100K | $0 (not in the Partner Program yet) | $0 | **$0** |
| Months 4–6 | ~300K | $150–450 | $50–200 | **~$200–650** |
| Months 7–12, if it goes well | 1–2M | $700–3,000 | $200–1,000 | **~$1K–4K** |
| Year 2, if one series hits | 5–10M | $4K–15K | $1K–5K plus sponsors | **~$5K–20K+** |

These are estimates, not promises. Many channels never get past the first two rows.
